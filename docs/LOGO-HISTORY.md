## 2. What the original actually is

Worth stating precisely, because the repository's own README gets it wrong.

`~/projects/good-ciso-logo/README.md` describes *"a stylized G within a hexagonal shield shape, with a small amber accent dot."* The pixels say otherwise, and the difference matters:

- The shield is **not hexagonal**. It is a **rounded shield** — a flat-topped, tapering form with a point at the bottom.
- The "G" is **not a letterform**. It is the **negative space** the teal ring leaves inside itself — a counter, not a glyph.
- The counter is **open at the lower right**, which is what makes it read as a G rather than a C or an O.
- The amber dot sits **at the centre of the G's aperture**, not at the centre of the shield.
- The shield is a **ring**: teal band, white interior. Not a solid shape.

Measured from the source:

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Artwork | 1024×1024, 61 KB, **JPEG despite the `.png` extension** |
| Shield | x 444–586, y 337–500 — 142 wide × 163 tall |
| Teal | `#00A896` (brand teal) |
| Amber dot | `#F5A623`, centre (515, 413), radius ≈ 11px |
| Counter | white, the shield's inner boundary |
| Wordmark | "Good" (heavier) + "CISO" (caps), dark navy `#030712`, below the shield |

## 3. The chain of mistakes

There were **three separate GoodCISO marks** before the original was restored, and the sequence explains itself:

**2025-02 → 2025-04: no identity at all.** The repo held stock platform logos (LinkedIn's "in", Twitter's bird) and a gradient texture. Nothing that was actually GoodCISO's.

**2025-02-20 → 2026-08-12: the AWARE mark, wearing GoodCISO's name.** The teal square with a white tick is AWARE's shield-and-check. It was installed as GoodCISO's favicon, as its PWA icons, and as its Apple touch icon. This is the cross-brand error the current guard exists to prevent — and it went undetected for roughly **18 months**.

**2026-05-20: an approximation of the real thing.** Someone hand-drew `favicon.svg` — correct shield shape, correct teal, but the centre dot is **teal instead of amber** and the counter is navy instead of white. It was *close*, which is worse than being obviously wrong: it looked deliberate.

**2026-11-18: the original restored.** `extract_logo.py` cuts the actual artwork.

I verified the current mark is a faithful cut rather than another approximation:

| Measure | Original artwork | Current `goodciso-shield.png` |
|---|---|---|
| Teal silhouette IoU | — | **0.960** |
| Amber dot, normalised position | 0.501 / 0.472 | **0.505 / 0.471** |

The shape is the original's and the dot is in the original's place.

## 4. Why the 2024 wordmark was retired

`Good_CISO_Wordmark.svg` is the most interesting find in the history, and the wrong thing to do with it would be to treat it as canonical.

It is a **vector "Good CISO"** — 346.8 × 70, 17 paths, black. But:

- **It contains no mark.** It is type only.
- **It was generated by Matplotlib 3.10.1**, not designed. The `dc:title` says so outright. A renderer emitted the glyph outlines and the file was committed.
- **It is referenced by nothing** — no HTML, no CSS, no Python, no Markdown in the repo.
- **It says "Good CISO" as one word.** The current brand is "GoodCISO" as the corporate name, with "Good" and "CISO" set as a lockup beneath the mark.

It is a **text lockup**, not a logo. Its one real merit is that it is a clean vector of the name, and it may be worth keeping as a *reference* for anyone setting the wordmark — but it must not be mistaken for the brand mark, and it must never ship as the site's identity.

## 5. Current state — is it right?

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is the mark on the site the original artwork? | **Yes** — IoU 0.960 on the silhouette, amber dot in the original's position |
| Is the wordmark the original's lettering? | **Yes** — "Good" heavier, "CISO" caps, in the artwork's navy |
| Does AWARE's green appear on any GoodCISO asset? | **No** — verified byte-comparison in `qa.py` |
| Does GoodCISO's teal appear on any AWARE asset? | **No** — same guard, inverse direction |
| Are the off-repo surfaces correct? | **Yes** — LinkedIn avatar and cover carry the teal mark, 0 green pixels |

**One gap, and it is a real one.** The wordmark is **live text**, not artwork. "Good" in bold + "CISO" in caps is set by the browser's font stack, so it renders differently on every machine. The original's letterforms are in the raster artwork but not in the lockup.

That is a deliberate trade — live text stays selectable, crisp at any size, and gives the accessible name for free. But it means **the wordmark is not the original's letterforms**. It is a faithful *interpretation*. If exact letterforms ever matter — a print piece, a letterhead, a vehicle — the original PNG carries them and the SVG cannot, because the source artwork is a JPEG raster.

## 8. Verification notes

Every claim above was checked against the artifacts, not inferred:

- **Asset extraction** — `git log --all --diff-filter=A` over all three repositories, then the adding commit's blob extracted and rasterised.
- **Silhouette fidelity** — teal mask IoU between the original artwork and `goodciso-shield.png`, both normalised to their bounding boxes: **0.960**.
- **Dot position** — amber centroid normalised against the shield's teal bbox: original 0.501/0.472, current 0.505/0.471.
- **Brand separation** — teal and green pixel counts per asset; the AWARE green is absent from every GoodCISO asset and vice versa.
- **Live state** — `https://goodciso.org/favicon.png` returns 200 at 75,683 b (the original artwork); `favicon.svg` returns 404, which is correct, because the approximation is retired.

The one claim I could not verify from the artifacts alone is *why* the 2025 assets were stock platform logos rather than GoodCISO marks. The repository records when they were added and removed, not the intent. The dates are consistent with a site assembled before any GoodCISO identity existed; that is an inference from the timeline, and it is labelled as such.

---

## 7. The governing rule, restated

> **One mark per brand, cut from one source of truth, verified by byte-comparison at build time.**

That rule is what `qa.py` now enforces across all four sites, and what `test_brand_guard.py` proves by breaking each condition on purpose. The eighteen-month window in which AWARE's tick was GoodCISO's favicon is the argument for it.
